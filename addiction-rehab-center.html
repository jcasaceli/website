Understanding Your Coverage

What Is NYSHIP — and Does It Cover Rehab?

The New York State Health Insurance Program (NYSHIP) is one of the largest public employer health plans in the United States, covering over 1.2 million state and local government employees, retirees, and their families across New York.

Under the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA), your NYSHIP plan is legally required to cover substance use disorder and behavioral health treatment at the same level as any medical condition. This is federal law — not a courtesy.

Translation: if you're struggling, your benefits are there. The question is just how to use them.

NYSHIP covers employees at:
  • New York State agencies (DOT, DOH, DOCCS, DMV, and all others)
  • SUNY & CUNY universities and colleges
  • County and city government offices
  • Public school districts across NY
  • Town and village government employees
  • Fire districts, water authorities, public libraries
  • Retirees and eligible dependents

The Empire Plan & NYSHIP HMOs

Most NYSHIP members are enrolled in The Empire Plan — the flagship NYSHIP plan covering the majority of state employees. Behavioral health benefits under the Empire Plan are robust and include all levels of addiction treatment.

Depending on your region, you may instead be enrolled in a NYSHIP-participating HMO. Either way, treatment is a covered benefit under your plan.

NYSHIP-participating plans include:

Levels of Care

The Right Level of Care, Covered by Your Plan

NYSHIP and the Empire Plan cover the full continuum of addiction treatment. Our clinicians will recommend the appropriate level — and we'll confirm your coverage before you begin.

Level 1

Medical Detox (Inpatient)

24-hour medical supervision through withdrawal. Essential for alcohol and benzodiazepine dependence, and helpful for opioids and kratom. The safest, most comfortable way to get through the first phase.

Level 2

Residential Treatment

Full-time structured care in a treatment environment. Ideal when home or work environments make early recovery difficult. Typically follows detox for moderate-to-severe addiction.

Level 3

Partial Hospitalization (PHP)

5-6 hours of daily programming, 5 days a week. Intensive enough for real progress, flexible enough to return home each evening. Often the step-down from residential.

Level 4

Intensive Outpatient (IOP)

3-4 hours of programming, 3-5 days per week — including evening options. Designed for working professionals. Many of our NYSHIP clients continue working during IOP.

Level 5

Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT)

FDA-approved medications — Suboxone, Vivitrol, Naltrexone — combined with counseling. Especially effective for opioid and alcohol use disorders. Covered by Empire Plan.

Level 6

Dual Diagnosis Treatment

When addiction and mental health conditions like depression, anxiety, or PTSD occur together — which they often do in high-stress professions — both must be treated simultaneously for lasting recovery.

Why Alcohol Withdrawal Requires Medical Supervision

Alcohol is one of the only substances where withdrawal itself can be life-threatening. Unlike opioid withdrawal (which is intensely uncomfortable but rarely fatal on its own), alcohol withdrawal can cause:

🚨 Seizures

Grand mal seizures can occur 6–48 hours after the last drink in people with significant alcohol dependence. Without medical supervision, these can cause serious injury or death.

🧠 Delirium Tremens (DTs)

DTs — the most severe form of alcohol withdrawal — includes hallucinations, extreme confusion, dangerous heart rate and blood pressure changes. DTs carry a mortality rate of up to 15% without treatment.

💓 Cardiovascular Complications

Alcohol withdrawal causes dangerous spikes in heart rate and blood pressure. People with underlying cardiovascular conditions face particularly elevated risk during unsupervised withdrawal.

😰 Severe Anxiety & Insomnia

Even without life-threatening complications, alcohol withdrawal produces extreme anxiety, insomnia, and discomfort that makes staying sober without support nearly impossible.

Benzo Dependency Among NY Government Workers

Anxiety disorders are common in high-stress public sector jobs. Teachers managing difficult classrooms, social workers carrying traumatic caseloads, corrections officers managing institutional violence, state agency workers navigating bureaucratic pressure — these are populations with genuinely elevated anxiety. Benzodiazepines are often appropriately prescribed. The problem develops slowly, as tolerance builds and the original prescription dose no longer works.

Never try to stop benzodiazepines without medical supervision. Benzo withdrawal can cause seizures and death. Unlike opioid withdrawal, which is miserable but rarely fatal, benzo withdrawal is in the same category as alcohol withdrawal — genuinely dangerous without a medically managed taper.

🚨 Xanax (Alprazolam)

One of the most prescribed — and most abused — benzodiazepines. Xanax's short duration of action makes physical dependence develop quickly, sometimes within weeks of daily use.

💊 Klonopin (Clonazepam)

Longer-acting than Xanax. Prescribed for anxiety, panic disorder, and sometimes seizures. Klonopin dependence can develop even in patients taking prescribed doses long-term.

😴 Valium (Diazepam)

Used for anxiety and sometimes prescribed as part of alcohol withdrawal treatment. Long half-life makes dependence less immediate but withdrawal management still requires supervision.

💤 Ativan (Lorazepam)

Commonly prescribed for anxiety and medical procedures. Ativan dependence is frequently seen in professionals who take it for work-related anxiety over extended periods.

Frequently Asked Questions — Benzo Treatment & NYSHIP

Does NYSHIP cover benzodiazepine detox?▾
Yes. Benzodiazepine detox is medically necessary and covered under NYSHIP behavioral health benefits. Like alcohol, benzo withdrawal can be dangerous — even fatal without medical supervision — making detox a critical covered service.
Why is benzo withdrawal so dangerous?▾
Benzodiazepines and alcohol affect the same receptor systems (GABA). Abrupt withdrawal from benzodiazepines after dependence can cause seizures, extreme anxiety, psychosis, and death. This is why cold-turkey benzo quitting is never recommended — medical supervision is essential.
What medications are used for benzo detox?▾
The standard treatment is a gradual taper using a longer-acting benzodiazepine (typically Valium/diazepam or Librium/chlordiazepoxide) combined with supportive medications for anxiety, sleep, and other symptoms. The taper is done slowly under medical supervision to minimize withdrawal severity.
I was prescribed Xanax by my doctor. Does NYSHIP still cover addiction treatment?▾
Yes. The source of the prescription does not affect coverage. Benzodiazepine dependence — whether from a legitimate prescription or misuse — is a covered behavioral health condition.
How long does benzo detox take with NYSHIP coverage?▾
Benzo detox is typically longer than opioid or alcohol detox due to the taper protocol — often 2–6 weeks for the medical component. NYSHIP covers the medically necessary duration of detox.

Loss of occupational identity, reduced social connection, chronic pain that was managed through prescription medications, and increased alcohol consumption are all more common among retirees. Many people who successfully managed substance use or dependency during their working years find it escalates after retirement. Your NYSHIP retiree coverage can help.

NYSHIP Retiree Behavioral Health Coverage

Empire Plan Retirees

Retired state employees who continue the Empire Plan have the same behavioral health coverage as active employees — including full addiction treatment. Coverage includes detox, residential, PHP, IOP, and MAT.

NYSHIP HMO Retirees

Retirees enrolled in NYSHIP HMOs (CDPHP, MVP, EmblemHealth, Excellus BCBS) have behavioral health coverage for addiction treatment under their plan's benefits.

Medicare + NYSHIP

Many NYSHIP retirees are also on Medicare. When Medicare is the primary insurer and NYSHIP is secondary, coordination of benefits may further reduce your out-of-pocket costs for treatment.

Retiree Dependents

Spouses and eligible dependents covered under a NYSHIP retiree's plan also have behavioral health coverage for addiction treatment.

Substances Commonly Misused by NY State Retirees

  • Alcohol — by far the most common. Retirement increases daily drinking opportunity and removes work-related constraints on drinking.
  • Prescription opioids — many retirees have chronic pain from careers in corrections, public works, or other physical occupations. Long-term opioid prescriptions create dependency.
  • Benzodiazepines — anxiety medications prescribed for retirement-related stress, anxiety, and sleep disruption can cause dependency.
  • Kratom — marketed as a natural supplement for pain and energy, kratom is popular among retirees managing chronic pain without wanting prescription opioids.
